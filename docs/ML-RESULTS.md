## Task and evaluation

Predict a driver's later clean-lap times in a held-out race. For each held-out race every method may use each driver's first 5 clean laps (the calibration window) and nothing else from that race, so the baselines and the model get the same information about it. The model is trained on the other races only (leave-one-race-out); the baselines' coefficients likewise come from the training races. Dry races only; sprints, wet races and laps flagged by the data-quality layer are excluded.

- Held-out races: 66; driver-race calibrations: 1211; predicted laps: 54082.
- Choices fixed before any result was seen: calibration window 5 laps, minimum 8 later laps per driver, LightGBM hyperparameters (below), 2,000 bootstrap resamples over races, 95% percentile intervals. No hyperparameter search was run.

## Lap-number effect estimated from the data (replaces the assumed fuel effect)

B1's lap-number coefficient is the change in lap time per lap of race progress after removing tyre age and compound. It is fuel burn plus track evolution plus anything else that changes with race progress, so it is not a pure fuel estimate. Intervals come from resampling races (500 draws).

- Lap-number effect: -0.0402 s per lap (95% CI -0.0470 to -0.0349); the dbt assumption is -0.04.
- Tyre-age slope SOFT: +0.0514 s per lap of age (95% CI +0.0333 to +0.0768).
- Tyre-age slope MEDIUM: +0.0166 s per lap of age (95% CI -0.0114 to +0.0503).
- Tyre-age slope HARD: +0.0217 s per lap of age (95% CI +0.0042 to +0.0448).

## Limitations

- Races are not independent draws, and a circuit appears once per season, so leave-one-race-out can train on the held-out circuit from another season (the model has no circuit feature; the leave-one-season-out table is the stricter check). 2023 to 2025 only; no claim for other regulations.
- The first 5 clean laps of a driver in a race are required, so drivers who retire early or never have enough clean laps are not scored.
- Lap-number and tyre-age effects are only separately identified through differences between stints (stint starts and compound changes).
- Hyperparameters were fixed, not tuned; a tuned model could do better or worse.
